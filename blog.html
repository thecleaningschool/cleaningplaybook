Start, Hire, and Scale Your Cleaning Business.

Practical guides written by someone who actually runs a cleaning agency — not someone who researched one. Every article covers the operational reality: real pricing numbers, word-for-word scripts, and systems that work from day one.

Starting a cleaning business is one of the most accessible paths to building a real income-generating operation — low startup costs, recurring revenue, and demand that doesn't disappear in a downturn. But the gap between knowing the model and actually executing it is where most people get stuck.

The articles on this blog are written from inside a real cleaning agency that's operating right now. Not adapted from YouTube videos, not assembled from research, and not written by someone who cleaned houses ten years ago and now sells courses about it. The pricing tables, phone scripts, and hiring frameworks here are the same ones used day-to-day in an active residential cleaning operation.

If you're figuring out how to start a cleaning business — the LLC setup, the tech stack, hiring your first cleaner before you run any ads — that's covered here. If you're trying to understand how much to charge for cleaning services without underpricing yourself out of a real margin, there's a full breakdown of what professional agencies actually charge in 2026. And if you've already got a few clients and you're wondering how to scale a cleaning business past $5K/month, the phase-by-phase roadmap is here too.

The most underestimated part of running a cleaning agency isn't finding clients or hiring cleaners — it's the phone call. Most leads are lost not because the price is wrong, but because the person answering doesn't have a script. A structured call process converts 40%+ of inbound inquiries into bookings. Without one, the same leads convert at half that rate or worse.

Use the articles below as a reference at whatever stage you're at. Each one covers a specific part of the cleaning business model in depth — with the actual numbers and operational detail that most free content leaves out.
