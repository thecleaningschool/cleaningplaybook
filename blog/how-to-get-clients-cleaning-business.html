How to Get Clients for a Cleaning Business (7 Channels That Actually Work)

A ranked breakdown of every client acquisition channel for cleaning agencies — with realistic timelines, cost estimates, and what to do first.

7 min read  ·  Cleaning Agency Playbook
Quick answer: The fastest way to get your first cleaning clients is a combination of Nextdoor/local Facebook posts (free, works in days) and lead platforms like Thumbtack or Angi (paid per lead, high buyer intent). Once you have a few clients, Google reviews become your most powerful long-term channel. Paid ads and SEO work — but take longer to build.

Getting clients is where most new cleaning businesses stall. Not because the market is too competitive — residential cleaning is a $100+ billion industry and demand for reliable services consistently outpaces supply. The real problem is that most owners try one channel, get inconsistent results, and assume marketing doesn't work for them.

It does work. But different channels operate on different timelines and require different inputs. This guide ranks each one honestly — what it costs, how fast it works, and when to use it.

1. Nextdoor and Local Facebook Groups

This is where to start. It's free, it's local, and it works within days. Nextdoor is particularly effective because people trust recommendations from neighbors — and a post about a new local business feels personal rather than like an ad.

What to post:

"Hey neighbors — I just launched a residential cleaning agency in [neighborhood/city]. We're offering 15% off for first-time clients this month while we build our local reviews. We're fully insured, our cleaners are background-checked, and we use professional-grade products. If you've been looking for reliable help at home, reply here or DM me."

Post in 3–5 local Facebook groups (neighborhood groups, "buy/sell/trade" groups, local moms groups) with the same message slightly varied. Do not post the identical text in every group — Facebook will flag it as spam.

Conversion tip: When someone replies, move the conversation to a phone call as fast as possible. Text and DM inquiries convert at a fraction of the rate of a short phone call. Have a script ready — even a basic one dramatically increases your close rate.

2. Lead Platforms: Thumbtack, Angi, and Yelp

Lead platforms send you homeowners who are actively searching for cleaning services right now. That intent is valuable — these are not cold prospects. They want to hire someone; the question is whether it'll be you.

The three main platforms for cleaning agencies:

  • Thumbtack — you set a budget and pay per lead received. High volume in most markets. Expect to pay $15–35 per lead.
  • Angi (formerly Angie's List) — slightly higher cost per lead but often higher-value clients. Works best in suburban markets.
  • Yelp — effective in urban markets where Yelp has strong penetration. Set a daily budget and monitor closely.

The most important rule with lead platforms: respond within 10 minutes. Research consistently shows that response speed is the single biggest factor in converting inbound leads. A lead that doesn't get a call within 10 minutes is usually already talking to your competitor.

Your goal on the first call is to book an appointment, not explain your entire service. Keep it short, sound confident, confirm availability, and close. A word-for-word phone script makes this significantly easier when you're just starting out and every call feels high-stakes.

3. Google Business Profile

If you don't have a Google Business Profile set up, stop what you're doing and create one today. It's free, and it's how you show up when someone in your city searches "house cleaning near me."

Setup takes about 20 minutes:

  1. Go to business.google.com and create your profile
  2. Choose "Service area business" (you go to clients, they don't come to you)
  3. Add your service area (city or zip codes you cover)
  4. Select "House Cleaning" and "Residential Cleaning" as your categories
  5. Add your phone number, website, and hours
  6. Write a 150-word description using natural language that includes "cleaning service in [city]"

You won't rank in search immediately — Google takes a few weeks to verify new profiles and several months to rank them well. But every review you collect during that period accelerates the process. Start collecting reviews from your very first clients.

4. Referrals and Google Reviews

Your existing clients are your best marketing channel — most owners ignore this completely. A happy client who tells two neighbors is worth more than $200 in advertising. And a Google review from a real customer drives more conversions than almost any other trust signal.

The system is simple:

  1. After the first clean, send a text: "Hi [name], this is [your name] from [agency]. Thanks for booking with us — hope everything looked great! If you have 60 seconds, a Google review would mean a lot to us: [link]. Thanks again."
  2. For clients who become regulars, ask for a referral directly: "Do you know anyone else in the area who might be looking for a reliable cleaning service? We're growing and referrals are how we find our best clients."
  3. Offer a small referral incentive — $20 off their next clean for every client they send your way.

A cleaning agency with 20 genuine Google reviews converts new visitors at 2–3x the rate of one with none. The reviews compound — the more you have, the higher you rank, the more leads you get without spending anything.

5. Facebook and Instagram Ads

Paid social ads work for cleaning agencies — but they work best once you have a few things in place first: a Google Business Profile with some reviews, a clear offer, and a phone number you actually answer.

Start with Facebook. The targeting options let you reach homeowners in specific zip codes, which is exactly what you need. A simple ad format that works:

  • Image: A clean, bright photo of a tidy room or a professional-looking cleaner at work
  • Headline: "Reliable House Cleaning in [City] — Book Online in 2 Minutes"
  • Body: "Licensed, insured, and background-checked cleaners. Satisfaction guaranteed. Starting at $[your price]."
  • CTA button: "Call Now" or "Get a Quote"

Start with $15–20/day and run it for two weeks. Watch which ad sets get calls, not just clicks — clicks are cheap, calls are revenue. Once you find a combination that produces bookings at a cost you can justify (under $80–100 to acquire a recurring client is usually profitable), scale it up.

6. Door-to-Door and Flyers

Old-fashioned, but effective as a launch tactic in a tight geographic area. Print 200–300 door hangers or flyers, target a specific neighborhood, and walk them yourself or pay someone $15/hour to do it.

What works on a flyer: your offer, your price (or starting price), your phone number, and one trust signal (insured, background-checked, etc.). What doesn't work: a long paragraph of text. Most people will glance at it for 2 seconds before deciding whether to keep it or toss it.

Target neighborhoods with homes in the $400K–$800K range — these households spend more on services and have higher trust in professional operators. Avoid apartment complexes (they often have cleaning staff already) and target single-family homes where the homeowner makes the purchasing decision.

7. SEO and Blog Content

This is the slowest channel to build but potentially the most valuable at scale. When someone in your city searches "best house cleaning service in [city]" or "how much does house cleaning cost in [city]," you want your website to appear.

Getting there requires three things:

  1. A real website with your location, services, and pricing clearly stated
  2. Google Business Profile (covered above) with consistent reviews
  3. Local content — even 3–4 blog posts targeting local search terms can dramatically improve your ranking over 3–6 months

For a new agency, SEO is not where to put your energy in months one and two. But if you set it up alongside everything else, it starts compounding quietly in the background while you grow through faster channels.

The Right Order to Attack These Channels

New owners try to do everything at once and end up doing nothing well. Here's the order that makes sense:

  1. Week 1: Post on Nextdoor and local Facebook groups. Get your first 1–2 bookings from free channels.
  2. Week 2: Set up your Google Business Profile. Create a Thumbtack account and buy your first leads.
  3. Week 3–4: Ask every client for a Google review. Start building your referral ask into every post-clean follow-up.
  4. Month 2: If you have consistent cleaners and a steady call response system, test $300–500 in Facebook ads.
  5. Month 3+: Keep optimizing what's working. Let reviews and referrals compound. Add SEO content.

The mistake isn't picking the wrong channel — it's trying to skip the fundamentals. A business with a responsive phone line, a professional offer, and 10 Google reviews will outperform one with $1,000/month in ads and nothing else in place.
