Cleaning Business Phone Script: Word-for-Word Guide to Booking More Clients

The exact script structure used in a real cleaning agency — covering the opening, pricing conversation, common objections, and close.

6 min read  ·  Cleaning Agency Playbook
Quick answer: Most cleaning businesses lose bookings on the phone — not because the price is wrong, but because the call feels uncertain and unstructured. A word-for-word script removes hesitation, builds confidence, and consistently converts 40%+ of inquiries into booked appointments. The script below is built from a real operating agency's incoming call process.

The phone call is where most cleaning business leads die. A homeowner finds you on Thumbtack, Google, or Nextdoor, picks up the phone ready to hire — and something about the call loses them. They say "I'll think about it" and never call back.

It's rarely the price. It's usually uncertainty on your end: a pause in the wrong place, answering a question you weren't expecting, or leaving the booking step vague at the end. A structured script eliminates all of that. You know exactly what to say, in what order, and what to do when someone pushes back on price.

This is the framework behind the script used in a real cleaning agency today — not a generic sales template adapted from another industry.

Why Scripts Work (And Why Most Owners Resist Them)

Most new cleaning business owners resist scripts because they think it'll sound robotic or forced. The opposite is true: a well-practiced script sounds natural and confident, because you're not making decisions mid-call about what to say next. You're just talking.

Compare how a doctor handles a patient intake versus how you'd answer the same questions cold. They ask the same questions every time, in the same order, because the structure frees them to actually listen. A script does the same thing for a sales call.

The goal of the call is narrow: gather the information you need to give a price, give the price confidently, handle any questions, and book the appointment. Everything else is noise.

The Incoming Call Script

Step 1: The Opening (First 10 Seconds)

How you answer immediately sets the tone. Sound professional, warm, and ready.

📞 You say:
"Thank you for calling [Agency Name], this is [Your Name]. How can I help you today?"
Tip: Never answer with just "Hello?" — it signals that you're not running a real business. Always lead with your agency name and your name. It sets a professional frame immediately.

Step 2: Gather the Details You Need to Quote

Before you give a price, you need two things: the size of the home and what type of clean they need. Ask these questions conversationally, not like a checklist.

📞 You say:
"Happy to give you a quote. Can I ask a couple of quick questions first so I can give you an accurate price?"
[They say yes]
"Great. How many bedrooms and bathrooms does the home have?"
[They answer]
"And are you looking for a standard recurring clean, or more of a deep clean — like if it's been a while since it was professionally cleaned?"
Tip: The "has it been a while" framing is deliberate. It lets the caller self-select into a deep clean without you making them feel judged for it. Deep cleans are higher-ticket and often easier to upsell once they're already on the phone.

Step 3: Give the Price Confidently

This is where most new owners hesitate — and hesitation kills bookings. Give the price directly, without apologizing for it or over-explaining it. Confidence in your pricing signals that your service is worth it.

📞 You say (standard clean example):
"For a [X] bed, [Y] bath home, a standard clean runs $[price]. That includes [briefly: bathrooms, kitchen, all rooms, floors, surfaces]. We bring our own professional-grade products, and all our cleaners are background-checked and insured."

State the price, then immediately add two trust signals (insured, background-checked) without pausing long enough for them to react. Then transition to availability:

📞 You say:
"We have availability [day] and [day] this week — which would work better for you?"
Tip: Offering two specific days (not "when are you free?") creates a low-friction choice. It moves the conversation toward booking instead of opening up an open-ended scheduling discussion.

Step 4: Handle the Common Objections

You'll hear the same objections on almost every call. Here's how to handle the three most common ones:

"That's a bit more than I expected."

📞 You say:
"I understand — I hear that a lot from people who've seen lower quotes online. The difference is usually insurance and reliability. A lot of the lower-priced options are solo cleaners without insurance, which creates a problem if anything gets damaged. We're fully insured, and if your cleaner ever cancels, we always send a replacement. For most clients that peace of mind is worth the difference."

"Can I get a discount for regular service?"

📞 You say:
"Absolutely — we do offer recurring client pricing. If you book biweekly service, the rate drops to $[price]. Most of our clients go that route because it's the best value and keeps the home consistently maintained."

"I need to think about it / talk to my spouse."

📞 You say:
"Of course, no pressure at all. Can I ask — is there anything specific you wanted to think over? Sometimes I can answer a question right now that makes it easier."
[If they still want to think about it]
"Totally fine. What's the best way to follow up with you — text or email? I can send over everything we discussed and hold that [day] slot for 24 hours if that helps."
Tip: Offering to hold a specific slot adds urgency without pressure. It makes the follow-up easier too — you have a reason to reach back out.

Step 5: The Close

When the caller is ready to book, confirm everything clearly and end with a concrete next step.

📞 You say:
"Perfect. I've got you down for [day] at [time]. You'll get a confirmation text with the cleaner's name and a reminder the night before. Payment is collected after the clean — we accept card on file or Venmo. Any questions before we wrap up?"
[Answer any final questions]
"Great — we'll see you [day]. Thanks for calling [Agency Name]."

The Follow-Up Text (For Leads Who Don't Book)

Not everyone books on the first call. Some genuinely need to check their schedule or discuss it with a partner. If you send a follow-up, do it within 2 hours while you're still fresh in their mind.

📱 Text template:
"Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] from [Agency]. Thanks for calling today — just following up on the $[price] quote for your [X] bed/[Y] bath home. We still have [day] and [day] available this week. Let me know if you'd like to go ahead or if you have any questions. No pressure either way!"

Keep it short, restate the specific quote, and offer the same two day options. Personalization (their name, their home details) converts significantly better than a generic check-in message.

The Biggest Phone Mistakes Cleaning Business Owners Make

  • Answering with "Hello?" — sounds like a personal call, not a business
  • Pausing before giving the price — signals uncertainty and makes the caller feel like the number is improvised
  • Saying "it depends" without a range — vagueness kills trust; give a number and explain it
  • Not asking for the booking at the end — many owners give a great call and then wait for the client to initiate; you have to close
  • Not following up — most leads need one touch after the first call; without a follow-up system, you're leaving money on the table

Practice Makes the Script Sound Natural

Read the script out loud five times before your first call. It will feel scripted at first — that's normal. By the third real call, you'll have internalized the structure and it'll feel conversational. By the tenth call, you'll be adapting it fluently to each caller's tone and pace.

The goal isn't to recite the script word-for-word forever. It's to build enough reps that the flow becomes second nature, so you can focus on listening rather than thinking about what to say next.
